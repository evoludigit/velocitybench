# FraiseQL vs the schema-to-API field — July 2026

**One rented box, one sweep, every number traceable.** FraiseQL v2.11 against
its true competitors (Hasura, PostGraphile) plus reference points (actix-web
as the hand-written ceiling, async-graphql / mercurius as fast Node/Rust
GraphQL), on Hetzner dedicated-vCPU hardware anyone can rent and re-run.

> **Interactive companion:** every chart below is live and explorable at the
> [VelocityBench site](../site/) — this page is the static, lift-into-a-post
> summary. Every figure here is the per-cell **median of three warm sweeps**
> and is also in the [full run JSON](hetzner-2026-07/bench-hetzner-2026-07-07-median.json)
> and [tabular report](hetzner-2026-07/bench-hetzner-2026-07-07-median.md) — no
> hand-typed numbers.

## TL;DR

Against the engines that, like it, turn a schema into an API with no
hand-written resolvers, **FraiseQL serves ~3× PostGraphile and ~6× Hasura on a
simple read (Q1), at a third to a sixth of their latency, in about a tenth of
their RAM.** Its edge *grows* with nesting depth (no N+1 by construction), and
it holds a flat tail. It does **not** beat a hand-written Rust server on trivial
flat queries, and its strong-consistency write path (full cascade) is
deliberately expensive — both shown here in plain sight.

## Who this is for

Two different comparisons live in one grid, and mixing them is the usual way
these benchmarks mislead:

- **Schema-to-API engines** — FraiseQL, Hasura, PostGraphile. You hand them a
  schema; they serve the API. This is the comparison FraiseQL is *in*.
- **Hand-written servers** — actix-web (REST), async-graphql, mercurius. A
  developer wrote the query and the serialization by hand. These are reference
  points — the ceiling and the fast-GraphQL baseline — not FraiseQL's category.

Both are shown, always labelled, never blended into one ranking.

## Visual 1 — Latency vs nesting depth (the architecture, as data)

RPS as the query nests deeper: a flat user list (Q1), a 2-level comment→author→post
join (Q3), a full blog page (T1). Resolver engines climb a per-level cost;
FraiseQL reads one pre-shaped row. → *live chart: [site §S1](../site/#s1-nesting-cliff)*

| Engine | Q1 | Q3 | T1 | Q1→Q3 |
|--------|---:|---:|---:|------:|
| **fraiseql-tv** (precompute) | **6 550** | **3 207** | **4 040** | −51% |
| fraiseql-v (on-the-fly views) | 6 451 | 1 303 | 2 114 | −80% |
| postgraphile | 2 099 | 1 177 | 1 658 | −44% |
| hasura | 1 060 | 820 | 656 | −23% |
| *actix-web (hand-written REST)* | *1 339* | *3 281* | *2 568* | *+145%* |
| *async-graphql (hand-written)* | *1 146* | *1 421* | *3 606* | *+24%* |

FraiseQL-tv stays 2.7–3.9× ahead of PostGraphile/Hasura across the depth series.
Its own `-v` variant shows the cost of composing JSONB per request (−80% by Q3)
— the honest conservative line.

## Visual 3 — MC1: mutation to consistent state (a *workflow* benchmark)

MC1 measures client round-trips to reach a consistent read after a write, **not
raw server speed**. FraiseQL returns the cascade in the mutation response (1
request/cycle); classical stacks re-fetch (2 requests/cycle).
→ *live: [site §S5](../site/#s5-write-trade) / [§S7](../site/#s7-amortization)*

| Engine | MC1 cycles/s | M1 (write) | M1d (delta write) |
|--------|-------------:|-----------:|------------------:|
| async-graphql | 1 025 | **6 480** | — |
| mercurius | 1 083 | 2 546 | — |
| postgraphile | 947 | 2 076 | — |
| **fraiseql-tv** | 89 | 89 | **6 783** |

This is the honest hard edge: FraiseQL's **full-cascade M1 is slow (89 rps)** —
every write recomputes ~61 precomputed rows for always-consistent reads. Its
**delta path (M1d, 6 783 rps)** patches surgically and competes with the fastest
writers. A write-heavy workload should pick a resolver engine or FraiseQL's
delta mode — the site's workload selector says exactly that.

## The Q1 credibility anchor — read honestly

Among **hand-written servers**, FraiseQL does not win the trivial flat cases:
actix-web serves Q2 far faster, and raw Rust will always beat a
compiled-from-schema engine on a query someone hand-tuned. That is the point of
showing them. Among its **own category**, FraiseQL tops Q1 outright — the four
FraiseQL variants take the top four Q1 spots — and its lead only widens with
nesting. The architecture (Python-authored schema, Rust runtime, no per-request
Python, no N+1 by construction) is the story; the RPS number is the evidence.

## Methodology

- **Hardware**: Hetzner CCX33 SUT (8 dedicated vCPU / 32 GB, AMD EPYC-Genoa,
  fsn1), PostgreSQL 17.10, kernel 6.8. Load generator k6 on a **separate**
  dedicated CPX42 box over a private network — never co-located.
- **Load-gen headroom**: a pre-sweep gate requires k6 to sustain ≥30 000 RPS
  against a null target on the SUT (measured 72 k, several × the fastest cell)
  — the numbers are server-bound, not load-generator-bound.
- **Same-run rule**: every figure is from one sweep on one box. Numbers are the
  **median of three warm sweeps**; the first post-seed sweep is discarded as a
  warm-up (cold-DB deadlock storm), and each cell's run-to-run spread is in the
  run JSON.
- **Logged tables**: FraiseQL's `tv_*` run LOGGED (crash-durable) — the
  conservative choice; unlogged would be faster but trades durability.
- **Trigger scoping**: `tview_trigger_scope = fraiseql-only` — pg_tviews
  cascade cost is charged only to FraiseQL's own runs.
- **MC1** is a workflow benchmark (round-trips to consistency), defined per
  engine family in the runner and generated into every report.
- **Versions**: FraiseQL 2.11.0, Hasura v2.49.3-ce, PostGraphile 5.0.3,
  actix/async/mercurius pinned from lockfiles at report time.
- Raw data: [median JSON](hetzner-2026-07/bench-hetzner-2026-07-07-median.json),
  [tabular report](hetzner-2026-07/bench-hetzner-2026-07-07-median.md),
  per-sweep JSONs and gate deltas alongside.
